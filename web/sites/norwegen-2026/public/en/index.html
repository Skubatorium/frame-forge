Eighteen days in the summer of 2026: the Skubatz family goes to Norway. One fully loaded estate car with a roof box, and a country that reinvents itself every few kilometres.

The trip had a fixed home and one long loop. We set off from Grevenbroich; our base was Skien in Telemark, staying with friends. After a few quiet days we headed out on a ten-day circuit across the west: Heddal, Geilo, down the Aurlandsfjellet mountain pass to the Aurlandsfjord, through the Nærøyfjord by electric boat, twenty kilometres in one go underneath a mountain, up to Geiranger, over the eleven hairpin bends of the Trollstigen and back across the Valdresflye plateau. Then one more stretch of skerry coast, fishing and quiet, before the ferry headed south again.

Two films came out of it, and they tell the story in very different ways. The Roadtrip Edition runs chronologically across all eighteen days, with brief notes on each leg: a kind of travel diary. The Drone Edition carries less information and lives entirely off the aerial footage: long shots from above, two pieces of music, nine and a half minutes of air. It is the landscape side of Norway.
